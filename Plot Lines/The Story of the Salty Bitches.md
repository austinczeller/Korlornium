# The Story of the Salty Bitches

*A record of the crew's voyage across [[Erodas]] — from the plague-ports of [[Duskwind Landing]] to the sandy shores of [[Thalmyre]].*

---

## The Crew

They did not choose each other. They were thrown together by a plague, a stolen ship, and a hunt across open water — and they stayed together long after any of those reasons remained.

**[[Vel'Korr]]** was a survivor of [[The Red Talons]], a piracy crew dismantled by a necromancer named [[Viros]] at [[Frostcrag Glade]]. The people he had sailed with, fought beside, and loved were dead — or worse than dead. He had been looking for Viros ever since.

**[[Faefi]]** was looking for Viros too, and for [[Mother Elyra]], who had been taken from an abbey and turned into something she was never meant to be. They made the hunt together, Vel'Korr and Faefi, for a long time before the rest of the crew found them.

**[[Picasso]]** carried a small companion named [[Sliver]] and a large, ancient problem he was still in the process of understanding. Something at the bottom of the ocean had been watching him for longer than he knew. It was hungry. It had missed him.

**[[Kel]]** was the son of innkeepers. His parents had run [[The Side Quest Tavern]] in [[Duskwind Landing]], and the tavern was gone — but Kel was still building things, still crafting, still quietly working out what he owed to a god called [[Forgas]] who seemed to have opinions about artisans.

**[[Thorne]]** was on her [[One-trek]], the journey that [[Heaven's Hole]] sends its people on when they are ready to know the world. She had not expected the world to be quite this much world.

**[[Soffalie]]** was a half-elf druid who had grown up in [[Duskwind Landing]]'s [[Driftwood Forest]] and watched it destroyed. She had opinions about nature, about justice, and about which creatures deserved to live. She brought the [[wok-wok|Wok-woks]] with her. The Wok-woks brought their own opinions about everything else.

---

## Part One: Plague, Ruins, and a Stolen Ship

The [[Sea Rot]] came first.

A disease swept through [[Duskwind Landing]], killing sailors and grounding ships. [[Borin Duskrag]], who ran the undercity, pointed the crew toward gnomes living in the [[Driftwood Forest]] who might know something. After interrogating them — and murdering one, which the rest of the crew considered somewhat expedient — they learned that the gnomes were innocent. Something dark was coming from [[Widow's Rook]]. They borrowed a boat, sailed out through the spice-addled fog, and stopped the mad wizard causing the plague. It was the most dangerous thing any of them had done.

Then they found the ruins.

The [[Old Duskwind ruins]] sat outside town, half-forgotten. Beneath them was something that had not been forgotten — an underground library sealed behind animated stone guardians. [[Vel'Korr]] was pulled into the earth by one of those guardians. He came back changed in a way he couldn't fully describe. Down in the library, the party met [[Zora Meadowlark]] for the first time: a woman who appeared frightened, then revealed something very long and very wrong, and teleported away before they could ask questions. They found books on an entity called [[Ulthagos]]. They found a box of beans.

The [[Driftwood Forest]] collapsed on their way out. [[Soffalie]] never forgot that.

The Duskwind town council, reasonably, suspected them. A man named [[Yannis Trask]] vouched for the party, invited them to dinner, drugged them, and had them thrown onto [[Saltmire Cay]] with a warning: *run before dawn.*

They did not run. [[Soffalie]] negotiated with a crocodile. They made camp. They waited.

What came next was [[Joanos]] — a former Red Talon, a face [[Vel'Korr]] had trusted, arriving at the head of a hunting party. The fight nearly killed them all. When it was over, [[Vel'Korr]] stood over his old crewmate and found his journal. The trail pointed to [[Zora Meadowlark]] and to a city called [[Dithoria City]].

They sailed back to [[Duskwind Landing]], cleared [[Father Elias Vance]]'s name (he had been manipulated, not complicit), and discovered that [[Yannis Trask]] had been dead for some time — a ghost operating a ship, running a trade in weapons that never arrived. In the chaos of figuring this out, [[Vel'Korr]] tried to speak with [[Joanos]]'s corpse using a necklace he'd found. He botched the questions. He learned almost nothing.

What they did learn was enough. The ledger from the captain's quarters showed travel between [[Dithoria City]], [[Frostcrag Glade]], [[Rosesholl]], and [[Stormveil]]. The same network. The same trail.

They walked out of the harbor on [[The Salty Bitch]] — a ship they had earned by surviving everyone who tried to kill them on it. [[Borin Duskrag]] argued about ownership. [[Vel'Korr]] argued back. The women sailed the ship home while the argument continued.

They recruited [[Morris Greentusk]], [[Felix Kled]], and a handful of others. [[Felix Kled]] agreed to carve a figurehead: a raven, at [[Vel'Korr]]'s request. [[Kel]] commissioned [[Stormwood]] armor. [[Soffalie]] received a pendant of speak with animals from [[Shandra Loomens]] and called it fair exchange for everything Duskwind had taken.

The first night at sea, [[Picasso]] saw a white squid with familiar eyes staring up at him from the dark water. Later that night, he dreamed of following it into an underwater cavern where [[Sliver]] transformed into the squid and devoured his tortle brethren. It turned to him and said:

*I AM SO HUNGRY.*

[[Picasso]] woke up sweaty. The crew sailed on.

## Part Two: Open Water, a Ghost Naga, and a Mayoral Election

The sea between [[Duskwind Landing]] and the rest of the world was not empty.

A Bone Naga named [[Nashirra]] found them on a burning island and delivered a message from her master: *bend the knee.* They declined. The naga served [[Zora Meadowlark]], who was still always ahead of them — and the conspiracy was larger than they'd understood. There were priests of [[Dithoria]] working the same network. There was something called [[The Ethereal Chain]] that bound a god and [[Void Rot]] beneath [[The Scar]], and people who very much wanted those chains broken.

They encountered [[Zora]] in disguise on the road, calling herself *Mira*, travelling with [[Sibil Meadowlark]] and a creature named Gork. She pointed them, without really admitting she was doing it, toward [[Stormveil]].

[[Stormveil]] was a logging town in the rain with an ancient [[Stormwood]] tree at its center and a mayoral election going very wrong. [[Terrance Highbrow]] had arrived from outside with money, rhetoric, and a plan to clear-cut everything that was left. [[Numa]] represented the people who had been there before the money arrived. On election night, doppelgangers with yellow eyes attacked in the crowd — clearly meant to frame [[Heaven's Hole]]. The party stopped them. [[Terrance]] blamed [[Heaven's Hole]] anyway.

The crew organized a recount, worked the [[The Miller's Union|Miller's Union]], built the case for alternatives — mud spas, owlbear tourism, trade routes that didn't require destroying the last Stormwood. [[Numa]] won. [[Terrance]] left. The ancient tree kept standing.

The road to [[Heaven's Hole]] required going through a swamp past a troll and an ogre. The crew handled them.

[[Heaven's Hole]] was old, quiet, and suspicious of outsiders. The [[The Community at Heaven's Hole|Deepseers]] — [[Deepseer Poppy]], Moon, Tragdar Windkeeper, Topaz — had kept this place safe for a long time. They gave the party water from the Hole. The water showed them things about themselves they hadn't asked to see.

Then [[Zora]] arrived properly, dropped the disguise, and talked.

She was being funded by a Dithoria fundamentalist sect that believed the god's sacrifice had been a mistake. The [[Gate Stone|Gate Stones]] she had been collecting were made of [[Ulthite]] — a material that slowed the spread of [[Void Rot]]. The Void Rot was real. It was spreading. And the next Gate Stone was being kept by a dragon named [[Nilah]], who was having problems of her own.

---

## Part Three: Nilah's Mountain

[[Nilah]] was a blue dragon, old and magnificent, and she was exhausted.

The Deepseers had taken one of her Gate Stones years ago and she had never stopped being aware of it. But the thing exhausting her now was the infestation — [[Void Rot]]-corrupted [[Gibberling|Gibberlings]], breeding in the caves beneath her lair and spreading purple crystalline rot through the mountain while she waited above for it to stop.

The crew made a deal: clear the infestation, take the Gate Stone.

They went down.

The cavern system was thick with void corruption — purple crystal growths on every surface, drow zombies wandering with empty eyes, something enormous and slug-like at the center of it all, birthing Gibberlings by the dozen. They fought through all of it. When the source was destroyed, the infestation began to die.

Nilah, clearer-minded than she had been in years, kept her word. She flew them to [[Heaven's Hole]] herself and watched them return the Gate Stone to the Deepseers.

[[Granite]], a resident of Heaven's Hole, gave [[Vel'Korr]] a dagger called [[Cutwater]] — magical, with advantage underwater, given in good faith after a rocky introduction. [[Thorne]] received [[Stillfall]], a legendary bow of pale Stormwood that looked grown rather than carved.

They set sail for [[Thalmyre]].

---

## Part Four: The Crossing

[[The Scar]] is open water — wide, wrong-feeling, and full of people who want what you have.

Midway across, [[The Drifting Bastards]] found them. Grappling hooks, a boarding crew, and [[Kobold|Kobolds]] flung onto the deck in a chaotic swarm that nearly killed [[Picasso]] in the confusion. The crew repelled them. From the aftermath they took a map of Thalmyre's perilous waters — the hidden routes through the reefs, the named rocks, the passages a crew that doesn't know the approach doesn't survive.

They found the route and navigated it. [[Tumbo]], the dock warden, met them on the jetty. [[Vel'Korr]] nearly gutted him on instinct. Tumbo defused the situation by blowing a raspberry on his belly.

He pointed them toward [[Whelk's Landing]].

---

## Part Five: Tidescrest

[[Whelk's Landing]] is built below the waterline, its walls thick old glass with the ocean floor on the other side. On the night they arrived, the ocean floor was bare. No crabs. No feeders. One fish drifted past, belly-up, slow.

[[Darla Murdinthe]] set drinks down without being asked.

The [[History/The Tidecrest Tournament of 1190|Tidescrest]] festival was everything [[The Seat of Thalmyre]] is when it tries to celebrate itself: greased masts, rum chugs, the Dead Man's Trail, fire-breathers, Goblin bookmakers, a Tiefling selling prophecies that all said the same thing. [[Captain Bubblebeard]] threw people across the fighting pit with the grin of a man who had never once considered losing. When they found him afterwards he told them to meet him in the morning — he had the [[Tidemother]] Gate Stone hidden somewhere safe and he would take them to it himself.

[[Kel]] found his parents' porridge on [[Darla Murdinthe|Darla]]'s menu. She'd been making it for years from a recipe out of [[Duskwind Landing]]. She didn't know whose it was. She just liked it. [[The Side Quest Tavern]] was gone, but the recipe had outlived everything.

[[Picasso]] did not make it to morning without incident. At the glass wall of [[Whelk's Landing]], something looked in at him from the dark water — one pale eye, vast, deliberate. Then the bar was gone. He was in open water, cold and absolute, and [[Cthulhu]] was in front of him: thrilled, like something that had been waiting by the door for a very long time.

It showed him the [[Tidemother]] dying — a god dimming at the center of a failing chain. It pressed something into his hand: a seed, dark and dense, warm in a way that had nothing to do with temperature.

*Don't lose it,* it said. *I've missed you.*

By morning, [[Captain Bubblebeard]] was dead in his chair — no wounds, glass eye removed. The festival had emptied. The people with fresh Dithoria runes on their skin were walking in silence toward the eastern docks. [[Darla Murdinthe]] was behind the bar when the crew arrived.

*"Take what you need,"* she said. *"Go fix it. Whatever it is."*

---

## Part Six: Coffin Rock

[[Coffin Rock]] is a narrow basalt island half a mile off the eastern shore. Sixty or seventy controlled festival-goers stood in a ring at the lip of a massive excavation cut into the top of the rock, silent, watching the hole.

The crew went down.

The chamber at the bottom was rough-hewn, salt-damp, and low-ceilinged. The [[Tidemother]] Gate Stone pulsed at the far end with a dark light that illuminated nothing. Behind it, standing at attention in red coats: [[Varnak]], [[Astra]], [[Joanos]], and three others. The [[The Red Talons|Red Talons]] — dead, reanimated, and waiting.

[[Viros]] stood in front of them.

He had the bearing of someone who had not been worried in a very long time and saw no reason to start. He didn't look up when the crew descended.

The [[Meadowlark Sisters|Meadowlark sisters]] were already in the chamber. [[Zora Meadowlark|Zora]] was speaking in a measured voice. [[Sibil Meadowlark|Sibil]] was standing slightly apart with her hand on her weapon. Viros had promised them something he had no intention of giving them.

*"This wasn't the arrangement,"* [[Zora Meadowlark|Zora]] said.

*"The arrangement evolved,"* [[Viros]] said.

[[Faefi]] pushed [[Viros]] into the pit with [[Captain Bubblebeard|Bubblebeard]]'s own hand.

The Gate Stone shattered. The chamber floor buckled. A sea dragon tore through from below — the Void Guardian, void-corrupted, ancient, in agony, free from the ritual that had been slowly destroying it. It had no idea who the crew was. It did not care.

[[Thorne]] planted a magic bean beneath it.

A sixty-foot pyramid erupted through the basalt and impaled the dragon where it surfaced.

[[Picasso]] took out his magic ink and painted [[Cthulhu]] onto the dragon's ruined skull.

He was answered.

---

## Epilogue

When the chamber went quiet, [[Vel'Korr]] carried [[Varnak]] and [[Astra]] out of the rock and burned them. The [[The Red Talons|Red Talons]] did not deserve the way they had been kept. They deserved fire.

[[Faefi]] found [[Mother Elyra]] on the Red Talon vessel, standing at the rail, staring out at the water. She didn't turn until [[Faefi]] called her name. When she did, she was present — for just a moment, recognizably herself. She looked at [[Faefi]]. She said she shouldn't have been there.

Then she was quiet again.

[[Vel'Korr]] and [[Faefi]] left with [[Zora Meadowlark]], bound for [[The Underdark|the underdark]] and whatever waits for them there.

The rest of [[The Salty Bitches]] stood on [[Coffin Rock]] in the salt air, alive and covered in various residues.

[[Viros]] was dead. The Gate Stone was gone. [[Cthulhu]] had a foothold in the world. The controlled folk on the rock were themselves again — confused, cold, missing days they couldn't account for.


---

*The Salty Bitches sailed twenty-one sessions across [[Erodas]]. They fought a wizard's dead, a pirate tournament, gods' servants, ancient corruptions, and several things that turned out to contain [[Gibberling|Gibberlings]]. They kept each other alive. They changed the world in ways they mostly intended and a few they didn't.*

*Their story lives on. Their story is the beginning of something larger.*
