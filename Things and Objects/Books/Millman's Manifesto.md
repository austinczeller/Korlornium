# [[Millman's Manifesto]]

## Description

A pamphlet-turned-book written and distributed by [[The Miller's Union]] in [[Stormveil]]. It was inspired by the words of [[Picasso]] disguised as "Gus."

---

## Text

*Published by the Miller's Union, Stormveil*

---

### A Spectre is Haunting Stormveil

A spectre is haunting [[Stormveil]] — the spectre of the honest worker. All the powers of the mill floor, the Permitting Office, and the counting rooms of [[Dithoria City]] have entered into a holy alliance to drive it out: the investors and their permits, the clerks and their rubber stamps, the luxury merchants of [[Cragbrook]] who have never held an axe.

Where is the worker who has not been called a troublemaker by those who employ them? Where is the woman who has not been pushed to the front desk when she could outwork every man on the floor? It is high time the millworkers of [[Stormveil]] publish their views openly before the world, and meet this tale of the spectre with a manifesto of the workers themselves.

---

### I. The Millworker and the Owner

The history of [[Stormveil]] is the history of the [[Stormwood]]. From the first axe laid to bark, it has been the backs of the people of this town that felled the trees, ran the saws, and loaded the shipments. The Stormwood does not cut itself. The timber does not walk to [[Dithoria City]] on its own legs.

For generations the Stormwood belonged to everyone who worked it. Then came the investors. Then came the permits. Then came men from behind desks in [[Dithoria City]] who have never smelled sawdust without a handkerchief pressed to their nose.

The millworker is reduced to a tool of production. Made appendage of the machine. It is not enough that they give their hours — the mill demands their hands, their backs, their years. When the machine takes something from a worker, management files a report. The mill does not mourn. It only demands more.

---

### II. The Owners and Their Kind

The owners did not build [[Stormveil]]. They found it. They found a town of workers already skilled, already organised, already turning [[Stormwood]] into something the world wanted. What they brought was capital and paperwork — and with paperwork came control.

Here is how it works. The owner arrives in town and sets the wage. He sets it low enough that the worker must return tomorrow, and the day after, and every day until the worker's body gives out. The worker has no choice but to return — for what is the alternative? The worker must eat. The worker's children must eat. The owner knows this. He has always known this. It is the foundation upon which his counting house is built.

And when the worker grows restless, the owner does not raise the wage. He raises a story. He tells the worker that the mill is struggling, that the margins are thin, that to ask for more is to threaten the livelihoods of everyone employed there. He makes the worker afraid — not of the owner, but of each other. He tells the [[Fowlmen]] that the [[Dwarf|Dwarves]] are why wages are low. He tells the Dwarves that the newcomers are taking their shifts. He turns the floor against itself, and then he returns to his counting house and tallies the week's shipments to [[Dithoria City]].

The Permitting Office stamps what the investors want stamped. The wealth of the [[Stormwood]] leaves on every cart. What stays behind is the mud and the injury reports.

And now they want [[Heaven's Hole]].

They have looked at a sacred place — a place the people of this region have known since before the permits existed, where the wood grows different and the veil grows thin — and they have seen board feet. They have seen shipments. They have seen the faces of wealthy men in [[Dithoria City]] light up at the grain of something they have never touched, and they have decided it belongs to them.

It does not belong to them.

---

### III. Who Holds the Axe

Let us be plain about something the owners do not wish spoken aloud.

They need us. We do not need them.

The owner brings capital. But capital does not fell a tree. Capital does not guide a saw blade or carry a beam or load a cart in the rain. Capital sits in a counting house and waits for the worker to do these things. Without the worker, the owner has a pile of coin and a forest he cannot touch.

Without the owner, the worker still has his hands. Still has her back. Still has the knowledge of the wood and the skill of the floor accumulated across years of labour. The worker knows how to run a mill. It is the only thing the owner does not know.

This is the secret the owners most fear becoming common knowledge: they are not the engine. They are the passenger. We are the engine. We have always been the engine.

What the owner provides is permission — permission to work the land, permission to sell the timber, permission to exist within the system he and his class designed. He charges dearly for that permission. He calls it investment. We call it what it is.

---

### IV. The Miller's Union

The [[Miller's Union]] does not ask for the impossible. We ask that a worker injured on the floor be compensated, not discarded. We ask that a woman who can outwork any man on the mill floor be permitted to do so. We ask that the [[Stormwood]] that sustains this town not be stripped to the root to fill the halls of people who do not know our names.

We are [[Fowlmen]] and [[Dwarf|Dwarves]] and [[Human|Humans]]. We came from the [[Agite Mountains]] and the farms of [[Caldrith]] and the forests of the [[Pugent Delta]]. We are different in every way except one: we all showed up to work this morning, and we will again tomorrow, and the morning after.

The owners have each other. They have the Permitting Office and the investors of [[Dithoria City]] and the merchants of [[Cragbrook]]. They have a system built across generations to protect their interests at the expense of ours.

We have each other. That is enough.

---

### V. What We Demand

The millworkers of [[Stormveil]] are owed the following — not as charity, but as consequence:

- That injury on the mill floor be the owner's responsibility, not the worker's misfortune
- That advancement within the mill reflect capability, not the accident of birth
- That no expansion of cutting permits reach [[Heaven's Hole]] without the consent of those who live beside it
- That the wealth of the [[Stormwood]] remain, in part, in the hands of those who cut it
- That the divisions sown among us by those who profit from our disunity be recognised for what they are — a tool of control, nothing more

---

*Workers of the mill, the cart, and the forest — you have nothing to lose but your poncho.*
