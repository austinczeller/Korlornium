# [[The Astral Volley]]

On the tenth night of [[Htaale (Heat)|Htaale]], the sky fills with light — streaks of silver and gold arcing across the dark in their hundreds. Somewhere up there, someone is shooting.

## Description

The Astral Volley is a three-night meteor shower that crosses the skies of [[Erodas]] each summer, beginning on the 10th of [[Htaale (Heat)|Htaale]]. The meteors fall in long bright arcs — fast, deliberate, relentless — and to the people of [[Erodas]] they have always looked less like falling stars and more like arrows loosed from an impossible bow.

The common belief held across most of [[Erodas]] is that the gods are shooting. At what, exactly, is a question people have argued about for centuries. Most traditions take it as a good omen — the gods are defending the world, clearing the sky of something unseen, making way for the harvest to come. A rich Volley of many bright streaks is taken as divine favor. A sparse or cloudy Volley is quietly worried over, though few say so aloud during the celebration.

## Observance

The Astral Volley is above all a revel. Bonfires are lit in fields, on beaches, and in town squares as soon as the sun goes down. People eat, drink, and make noise — the celebration is loud by tradition, matching the energy of the sky above. Music is expected and competition is encouraged; it is common for communities to hold informal contests of song, archery, and storytelling across the three nights.

It is considered good luck to spot the first streak of each night before anyone else in your group. Children count how many they see; whoever counts the most is said to have a fortunate year ahead. Pointing directly at a falling arrow is considered deeply bad luck — you do not want whatever is being aimed at to notice you.

## Regional Variations

- In [[Korlornium]], the Astral Volley coincides with the active fishing and farming season. Coastal communities take it as a sign of good waters and good harvests — fishers leave offerings at the waterline before the first night.
- In [[Thalmyre]], the Volley is one of the few occasions where open hostility between rival pirate crews is considered ill-omened. Most of the isles hold open feasts across all three nights. [[Whelk's Landing]] is said to be standing-room only.
- In [[Dithoria City]], scholars record the shower in precise detail each year and maintain archives going back centuries. The question of which gods are shooting, at what, and whether they are winning, is a subject of ongoing academic dispute.
- In [[Siroth]], where the night sky is vast and unobstructed, the Volley is visible with unusual clarity. [[Cragbrook]]'s upper districts host rooftop gatherings; in [[The Flats]], people press into the streets and the celebrations run until dawn on all three nights.
- In [[Goghdor]], the Astral Volley is the most significant event of the year. From above the cloud line the shower is unobstructed and overwhelming — hundreds of streaks per hour at the peak, visible with a clarity that those below the clouds cannot imagine. The Star Touched do not interpret the meteors as arrows or seeds. To them the Volley is the constellations speaking at full volume, and the [[Skoldrún]] of those who witness it from the summit become briefly and intensely vivid — more solid, more present, as though the sky is feeding them directly. It is the most common time for Star Touched to make their **Ascent**, believing the Volley makes the constellations more willing to answer. Communities that are otherwise autonomous gather on the upper slopes for the three nights, the only occasion in the year where most of [[Goghdor]]'s people are in the same place at the same time.
- In [[Seivara]], the Astral Volley carries a different interpretation — the arrows are not the gods' but the Ara's, a reminder that the spirits of the land are also the spirits of the sky. The elves and pixies of [[Seivara]] mark the Volley with quiet reverence as much as celebration, leaving offerings at sacred groves before the first night falls.

## Origins

The Astral Volley predates the [[The Erodian Standard Calendar|Erodian Standard Calendar]] and appears in the oldest traditions of most cultures across [[Erodas]]. Its name varies by region and its meaning shifts — some see protection, some see war, some see a game the gods play when they are bored. What is consistent across otherwise unconnected peoples is the impulse to go outside, look up, and feel something.

Whether the gods actually intend anything by it is, as ever, a matter of faith.
